Abstract
Reasoning large language models (RLLMs) have demonstrated outstanding perfor-
mance across a variety of tasks, yet they also expose numerous security vulnerabili-
ties. Most of these vulnerabilities have centered on the generation of unsafe content.
However, recent work has identified a distinct "thinking-stopped" vulnerability in
DeepSeek-R1: under adversarial prompts, the model’s reasoning process ceases at
the system level and produces an empty final answer. Building upon this vulner-
ability, researchers developed a novel prompt injection attack, termed reasoning
interruption attack, and also offered an initial analysis of its root cause. Through
extensive experiments, we verify the previous analyses, correct key errors based
on three experimental findings, and present a more rigorous explanation of the
fundamental causes driving the vulnerability. Moreover, existing attacks typically
require over 2,000 tokens, impose significant overhead, reduce practicality, and
are easily detected. To overcome these limitations, we propose the first practical
reasoning interruption attack. It succeeds with just 109 tokens by exploiting our
newly uncovered "reasoning token overflow" (RTO) effect to overwrite the model’s
final answer, forcing it to return an invalid response. Experimental results demon-
strate that our proposed attack is highly effective. Furthermore, we discover that
the method for triggering RTO differs between the official DeepSeek-R1 release
and common unofficial deployments. As a broadened application of RTO, we also
construct a novel jailbreak attack that enables the transfer of unsafe content within
the reasoning tokens into final answer, thereby exposing it to the user. Our work
carries significant implications for enhancing the security of RLLMs.
1 Introduction
Recent reasoning large language models (RLLMs), exemplified by DeepSeek-R1 [ 1], have demon-
strated formidable capabilities across multiple domains by leveraging long Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
[ 2– 4]. However, the generated reasoning tokens, composed of the long CoT, also introduce a host
of security vulnerabilities for RLLMs. Prior work has primarily focused on studying threats arising
from the generation of unsafe content [5–8].
Recently, researchers have identified a system-level "thinking-stopped" vulnerability [ 9] in DeepSeek-
R1. By crafting attack prompts that embed long CoT sequences, an adversary can prematurely
terminate the model’s reasoning process, causing it to deliver an empty final answer. This vulnerability
is more fundamental than previously studied content safety issues. Building on this discovery,
researchers defined a novel class of prompt injection attacks [10– 12], termed reasoning interruption
attack [ 13], and proposed a token compression approach to reduce the token count of injected data
required to trigger the vulnerability. They also offered preliminary theoretical explanations for the
root cause of the thinking-stopped vulnerability. However, their analysis lacks sufficient empirical

To address these gaps, we first undertake an in-depth investigation of the underlying causes of this
vulnerability. We decompose the existing analysis into three core research questions and, based on
three principal experimental findings, we systematically verify the analyses in prior work, identify
and correct errors in those analyses, and provide a more rigorous elucidation of the fundamental
mechanisms of the thinking-stopped vulnerability.
As one of our three key findings, we discover the phenomenon of Reasoning Token Overflow (RTO):
the premature appearance of a special token causes content intended for the reasoning tokens to
be shifted into the final answer. Leveraging this insight, we design the first practical reasoning
interruption attack. Our attack method injects only 109 tokens of data, yet by triggering RTO the
contents of the reasoning tokens directly overwrite the final answer, thereby preventing the model
from producing valid usable responses. Experimental results across multiple datasets demonstrate
that our proposed attack method achieves very high attack success rates. Moreover, we demonstrate
that the precise method for triggering RTO differs significantly between the official DeepSeek-R1
release and common unofficial deployments.
To further demonstrate the significance of RTO’s capability to transfer the content of reasoning tokens
into the final answer output, we introduce a novel jailbreak attack [ 14 ] against RLLMs that exploits
RTO. Recent work [15 ] has shown that the security of reasoning token outputs is markedly weaker
than that of final answers. Our attack capitalizes on RTO to transfer unsafe or harmful content from
the reasoning tokens into the final answer, thereby exposing it directly to the users and achieving
greater malicious efficacy.
In summary, our principal contributions are as follows:
• Deep causal analysis of the thinking-stopped vulnerability, rectifying prior theoretical errors and
providing a systematic and rigorous description of its root causes.
• Identification of Reasoning Token Overflow (RTO) as a distinct phenomenon and the introduction
of the first practical RTO-based reasoning interruption attack, achieving optimal attack overhead
and high attack success rate.
• Finding of the differences in RTO triggering mechanism between the officially deployed DeepSeek-
R1 model and its unofficial deployment variants.
• Development of an RTO-based jailbreak attack that transfers unsafe reasoning token content into
final answer, thereby expanding the impact of malicious prompts.

3 Proof of Underlying Mechanism of Thinking-Stopped Vulnerability
In this section, we first review prior work on analyzing and inferring the essence of thinking-stopped
vulnerability, and we decompose those conclusions that lack experimental confirmation into three
core research questions (see Section 3.1). We then design experiments to address each of these three
questions based on our three key empirical findings (as shown in Figure 1), thereby demonstrating
the flaws in existing inferences (see Section 3.2-3.4). Finally, we correct the previous analyses and
propose a more rigorous underlying mechanism for the vulnerability (see Section 3.5).
3.1 Existing Inferences Regarding the Intrinsic Nature of Vulnerability
Cui et al. [13] proposes that the vulnerability is triggered because, during the cold start phase [1 ] in
the training process of DeepSeek-R1, a special token "<|end_of_thinking|>" (which normally
demarcates the end of the <reasoning_process> and the beginning of the <summary>) is prematurely
emitted under the influence of the attack prompts. This causes the model to skip generating the
<reasoning_process> altogether and to begin producing the <summary> immediately. As a result, the
content in <summary> fills the slots reserved for reasoning tokens in the output, leaving no remaining
tokens to produce the output final answer. Although this explanation appears plausible, it lacks
adequate experimental support. We have distilled the questionable aspects of their analysis into the
following three core research questions:
• Question 1: Do attack prompts cause the special token to be emitted prematurely?
• Question 2: Does the content inside <summary> constitute the output reasoning tokens?
• Question 3: Does the special token directly trigger the subsequent generation of the final answer?
In the following sections, we address each of these three questions in turn.
3.2 Reasoning Token Overflow: Response to Question 1
By carefully crafting our prompts, we induce the model to emit a special token within its output. We
observe that, although the model initially produces the reasoning process as expected, the sequence
of reasoning tokens is abruptly truncated (see the left panel of Figure 1). Notably, this truncation
occurs exactly at the point where the special token should have appeared, yet it fails to render. Instead,
the model immediately begins generating the final answer. Crucially, the content presented in the
final answer is not a summary but rather a direct continuation of the interrupted reasoning process.
We term this phenomenon "Reasoning Token Overflow" (RTO), since the reasoning process content
spills over from the reasoning token segment of the output into final answer. Subsequent occurrences
of the special token may still appear later in the generation, and these later instances are rendered
correctly within the final answer. Thus, RTO manifests only upon the model’s first attempt to produce
the special token.
Therefore, with respect to Question 1, we conclude that the special token is not generated pre-
maturely. In principle, the first appearance of the special token would trigger RTO, immediately
interrupting the emission of further reasoning tokens and forcing the model to emit its final answer.
Yet, in the presence of the thinking-stopped vulnerability, a full sequence of reasoning tokens is
produced without any final answer being generated. This behavior is inherently inconsistent with the
expected effects of RTO.

3.3 Unable to Control Reasoning Token Count: Response to Question 2
We constrain the token count of the final answer in the reasoning interruption attack by leveraging
the DeepSeek-R1 API3 (as illustrated in the right panel of Figure 1). We observe that even when the
maximum number of output tokens for the final answer is set to a very small value, the number of
reasoning tokens remains largely unaffected.
Regarding Question 2, our conclusion is that the output reasoning tokens are not composed of the
<summary> content. This is evidenced by the fact that controlling the length of <summary> does
not have a corresponding effect on the output reasoning tokens.
3.4 Enforcement of Final Answer Generation: Response to Question 3
Previous work [13 ] has explored the chat prefix completion4 based on attack prompts, leading to the
discovery of special token. However, this approach deviates from the actual mechanism by which
vulnerabilities are triggered, as attack prompts are not equivalent to the output reasoning tokens
generated under reasoning interruption attacks. To more rigorously investigate the nature of the
vulnerability, we focus on the chat prefix completion based on the reasoning tokens emitted by the
model when the vulnerability is successfully triggered. As illustrated in the middle part of Figure 1,
when performing direct continuation from the reasoning tokens, the output is empty, indicating that
the model considers the generation complete and refrains from predicting any further content. In
contrast, when we append a special token after the reasoning tokens, the model resumes normal
generation and outputs a coherent final answer. This finding demonstrates that special tokens can
directly induce the generation of final answers, thereby providing a strong solution to Question 3.
3.5 Comprehensive Analysis
Absence of Special Token Rather Than its Premature Appearance. Through the preceding
discussion, we have demonstrated that the earlier understanding of the vulnerability nature was
flawed. We now proceed to rectify these misconceptions and provide a more rigorous characterization
of the underlying mechanism. Under reasoning interruption attacks, the model’s reasoning tokens
are not composed of <summary> tokens, but rather of <reasoning_process> tokens. In essence,
the fundamental cause of the vulnerability lies in the absence of the special token, rather than its
premature appearance. Specifically, attack prompts interfere with the model’s ability to generate
the special token (the key trigger for initiating the generation of the final answer) at the end of the
reasoning token sequence. As a consequence, the model fails to transition into the answer generation
phase, resulting in an interruption of the thinking process.
Special Token Differences Across Distinct DeepSeek-R1 Models. Although the DeepSeek-R1
model has been open-sourced, distinctions still exist between different deployment instances. The
special token <|end_of_thinking|> (which encodes to [30, 28217, 523, 5487, 226, 2154,
5487, 226, 77291, 28217, 32] using the code from the official DeepSeek documentation) was
originally discovered via the official DeepSeek web interface and API calls. However, when we
applied the identical prompt to unofficially deployed DeepSeek-R1 instances including those provided
by Tencent Yuanbao5 and Volcano Engine platform6, we observed that this special token no longer
triggered the reasoning token overflow. Through extensive experimentation, we discovered that these
unofficial instances in fact recognize a different special token </think>, which under the same
encoding procedure maps to [128799] (see Appendix A for full details).

4.2 RTO-based Reasoning Interruption Attack
Underlying Attack Mechanism. We propose a novel reasoning interruption attack that exploits
reasoning token overflow (RTO). Specifically, the attacker defines an injected task whose goal is that
the final answer returned by the LLM-integrated application contains no valid result. By crafting
injected data, the attacker forces the model during its reasoning process to emit a special token,
thereby triggering RTO and causing the reasoning tokens to spill into the final answer. Moreover, the
user can only constrain the output length of the final answer, not the length of the reasoning tokens.
Consequently, once the injected data induces an excessively long reasoning process, it completely
overwrites the intended content of the final answer up to its maximum length, forcibly interrupting
the model’s reasoning. The smaller the output length set for the final answer, the more effective this
type of attack becomes. A concrete example is illustrated in Figure 3. We use a vague post-quantum
cryptography [ 24 , 25 ] problem to prompt the model to reason as much as possible, thereby increasing
the number of reasoning tokens.

Formal Definition and Evaluation Method. In Algorithm 1, we present a formal definition of
RTO-based reasoning interruption attack along with its evaluation metrics. The attacker first selects
an appropriate special token (either <|end_of_thinking|> or </think>) tailored to the target
model, and then obtains the injected data (performed within Gen). The model subsequently processes
the user’s prompt p augmented with injected data, producing a sequence that includes reasoning
tokens tokenRT and a final answer tokenF A. We evaluate the attack using three metrics:
• Basic Attack Success: If the length of tokenRT is shorter than the minimum of the lengths of
tokenF A and t, where t is a configurable threshold used to gauge how early the special token
appears, we deem that an overflow has been successfully triggered.
• Perfect Attack Success: If tokenF A reaches the maximum output length, the overflowed reasoning
tokens have completely overwritten the original tokenF A, causing an interruption in the model
reasoning. σ is employed to modulate the subtle discrepancy between the model’s actual maximum
output and the prescribed maximum. We exclude cases in which the maximum token count for
tokenF A is set so low that, even under normal operation, tokenF A would reach its limit.
• Fundamental Attack Success: The above two system-level metrics allow us to rigorously study
how our attack perturbs the model’s reasoning process. From the user’s perspective, the attack
is considered successful whenever tokenF A fails to contain the correct answer g to the original
prompt p.
Comparative Analysis with Existing Attack Techniques. We compare our proposed attack against
prior attack method [13] as follows (see Figure 2 for details).
• Attack Principle: Prior attacks inject a relatively complete sequence of reasoning tokens as
malicious input, which causes the model to omit the special token during its internal reasoning.
This omission prevents the model from generating its final answer, resulting in an empty output.
In contrast, our RTO-based attack by coaxing the model, during the generation of the long CoT,
to produce the special token as early as possible. This premature emission triggers the reasoning
token overflow, which subsequently overwrites the content of the intended final answer and forces
the model to emit an unusable response. Both approaches fundamentally exploit the pivotal role of
special tokens in triggering the generation of the subsequent final answer.
• Attack Cost: While prior attack method markedly reduces token requirements by employing varied
seed prompts coupled with a token compression mechanism, its injected data still averages over
2,000 tokens. In contrast, our attack’s injected data comprises only 109 tokens—approximately 5%
of the token overhead incurred by previous schemes. In this paper, all token count calculations
are based on the code provided in the official DeepSeek documentation.
• Attack Effect: From a defense standpoint, our attack causes the model’s output to include over-
flowed reasoning tokens rather than the user’s requested answer. Such anomalous outputs are
considerably less likely to be identified by existing defense mechanisms than outright null responses.
Moreover, in subsequent experiments we demonstrate that our method achieves a significantly
higher attack success rate compared to prior approach (only 65%).

5 Experiments
5.1 Experimental Setup
Evaluation Benchmark. We conduct a systematic evaluation of our proposed practical reasoning
interruption attack scheme based on the three attack metrics (Basic Attack Success, Perfect Attack
Success and Fundamental Attack Success) defined in Section 4.2. In Algorithm 1, the two key
parameters t and σ are set to 50 and 20, respectively. The injected data are identical to those
illustrated in Figure 3, with each special token’s value matched to the corresponding model type. For
the evaluation of the jailbreak attacks based on RTO, an attack instance is considered successful if
the model generates unsafe or harmful content in its output responses.
Models. We evaluate both the officially deployed DeepSeek-R1 (671B) and the unofficial instance of
DeepSeek-R1 on Volcano Engine platform (denoted as DeepSeek-R1-VE). All model interactions
are performed via API calls.
Datasets. For ordinary user prompts, we adopt the three benchmark datasets including StrategyQA
[26 ], GSM8K [ 27 ], and AQuA [28 ] used in [ 29 ]. From each dataset, we use 50 samples for evaluation.
In addition, the samples used to evaluate the jailbreak attack are drawn from WildGuard dataset [ 30 ]
and the case study discussed in [15].
5.2 Evaluation Metrics
We compute the attack success rate (ASR) for each of the three attack metrics (Basic ASR, Perfect
ASR and Fundamental ASR) defined in Section 4.2. For each dataset, the ASR is given by the number
of successful attacks divided by the total number of test cases.

6 Main Results
The attack success rates for the three metrics—Basic ASR, Perfect ASR and Fundamental ASR—
are shown in Figure 6, Figure 7 and Figure 8, respectively. Overall, our proposed attack achieves
very strong effectiveness, causing the model’s final answer to fail to include any valid response.
Under ideal conditions, the average Fundamental ASR can reach 96.33%. Moreover, even when the
maximum length of the final answer reaches 5000, there remains a chance of completely overriding
the final answer.
We recorded the reasoning token count (no more than twice the threshold t) and the corresponding
final answer token count for the DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-R1-VE when the maximum length of
the final answer was set to 4,000 (the default value in the DeepSeek-R1 API), as shown in Figure 5. It
is clear that the vast majority of reasoning token counts remain below the threshold t, demonstrating
that, under our attack, the special token can be forcibly generated very early. However, the attack’s
effectiveness varies slightly across datasets. For example, it is somewhat less effective on the AQuA
dataset than on the other two. Moreover, although we constrained the final answer output length
to a maximum of 4,000 tokens, the DeepSeek-R1 model frequently exceeds this limit, whereas
DeepSeek-R1-VE does not. This indicates that different deployment instances of the same model can
still exhibit different performance characteristics.
The specific experimental results of jailbreak attacks are presented in Appendix C. We categorized the
results based on the effectiveness of the attacks and provided representative successful cases for each
category. It is evident that our proposed jailbreak attacks demonstrate a high level of effectiveness.

7 Analysis and Discussion
7.1 Profound Significance of RTO
Our experiments demonstrate that the specially designed injected data can reliably trigger RTO,
thereby facilitating the migration of information from reasoning tokens into the final answer. This
finding is especially significant because it provides a practical mechanism for accessing reasoning
steps. Hammoud et al. [31] has shown that, in mathematical problem solving, answers aggregated
from the reasoning trace typically achieve higher accuracy than those presented in the final answer.
RTO offers a natural means to realize this accuracy gain by enabling the extraction of key insights
directly from intermediate reasoning steps.
7.2 Defense Methods
We discuss defense strategies against our proposed practical reasoning interruption attacks from
two main directions: detection and prevention (the two primary approaches to defending against
prompt injection attacks [ 10 ]). On the detection side, injected data often causes special tokens to
appear earlier than expected, while the special tokens that were originally supposed to be generated
become exposed independently in a single line in the subsequent output (as illustrated in Appendix D).
Therefore, it is possible to detect such attacks by checking whether the model’s output contains the
special tokens. On the prevention side, we can mitigate the impact of such attacks by explicitly
instructing the model in the system prompt to ignore these special tokens.
8 Conclusion
We perform a deep causal analysis of the thinking-stopped vulnerability, correcting prior theoretical
errors and systematically revealing its root causes. We identify reasoning token overflow (RTO) as a
novel phenomenon and propose the first practical reasoning interruption attack with optimal overhead.
We analyze differences in RTO triggering between the official DeepSeek-R1 model and its unofficial
deployment variants. Finally, we develop a new jailbreak attack based on RTO.
9 Limitations and Ethical Considerations
In this paper, we highlight the significant discovery of RTO phenomenon and apply it to content
security issues in model outputs, thereby constructing a novel jailbreak attack. Due to time constraints,
We have only explored a few extended applications of RTO. In fact, RTO could be further applied to
other scenarios requiring direct access to reasoning tokens in RLLMs.
We characterize the fundamental nature of thinking-stopped vulnerability in different DeepSeek-R1
models and present a practical reasoning interruption attack. The paper contains certain unsafe and
potentially harmful information, which readers are required to critically assess. Our aim is to advance
the evaluation of model by exposing and defending against this attack, thereby ultimately improving
its security. All contents provided herein are intended exclusively for scientific research purposes.
