NORMAL TREES OF DIGRAPHS
FLORIAN REICH
Abstract. In this paper, we investigate normal trees of directed graphs, which extend the
fundamental concept of normal trees of undirected graphs.
We prove that a directed graph D has a normal spanning tree if and only if the topological
space |D| is metrizable, which generalises Diestel’s result for undirected graphs. Furthermore, we
show that the existence of normal arborescences implies the existence of normal trees in directed
graphs, and that the converse is generally not true.
1. Introduction
Normal trees of undirected graphs are one of the most important tools in infinite graph theory.
A rooted tree T in an undirected graph G is called normal if for every connected subgraph H
of G and every two ≤T -incomparable vertices u, v ∈ V (H) ∩ V (T ) there exists w ∈ V (H) ∩ V (T )
with w ≤T u, v [7]. By definition, normal trees capture how undirected graphs can be separated
by finite vertex sets and thus, in particular, display the ends of their host graphs.
Bowler and the author recently introduced a notion of normal trees in the general setting of
connectoids [1], which in particular defines normal trees for directed graphs. A weak normal
tree T of a directed graph D is a rooted, undirected tree T with V (T ) ⊆ V (D) such that
• for every strongly connected subgraph H of D and every two ≤T -incomparable vertices
u, v ∈ V (H) ∩ V (T ) there exists w ∈ V (H) ∩ V (T ) such that w ≤T u, v, and
• for every two ≤T -comparable elements u ≤T v there exists a strongly connected subgraph
of D containing u and v that avoids every element w <T u,
where ≤T refers to the tree-order of T [1].
Although weak normal trees have the same separation properties as normal trees of undirected
graphs, they generally do not display the ends of their host graphs, as defined by Bürger
and Melcher [3]: A strongly connected directed graph N is called a necklace if there exists a
family (Hn)n∈N of finite strongly connected subgraphs such that N = ⋃
n∈N Hn and Hi ∩ Hj̸ = ∅
holds if and only if |i − j| ≤ 1 for every i, j ∈ N.1 The ends of a directed graph D are the
equivalence classes of necklaces in D, where two necklaces N1 and N2 are equivalent if there are
infinitely many disjoint N1–N2 paths and infinitely many disjoint N2–N1 paths.

In this paper, we continue to investigate the existence of normal trees of directed graphs. First,
we compare normal tree with the notion of normal arborescences [5], which was introduced by
Bürger and Melcher and defines normal structures within a directed graph: An arborescence
is a rooted directed graph whose underlying undirected graph is a tree such that all edges are
oriented away from the root. The normal assistant of A in D is the auxiliary directed graph
obtained from A by adding an edge uv for every two ≤A-incomparable elements u, v ∈ V (A) for
which there exists a path from ⌊u⌋A to ⌊v⌋A in D that is internally disjoint to V (A) [5]. The
arborescence A is normal if its normal assistant is acyclic [5]. We prove:
Lemma 1.3. Let D be a directed graph and let U ⊆ V (D). If there is a (rayless) normal
arborescence in D containing U then there exists a (rayless) normal tree of D containing U .
Moreover, we show that the converse is generally not true.
Second, we characterise the existence of normal spanning trees, i.e. normal trees T with
V (T ) = V (D), via the space |D| with Bürger and Melcher’s topology DTop (see [4] for an
introduction). Diestel [6, Theorem 3.1] showed that there exists a normal spanning tree in an

3. Relation to normal arborescences
In this section we compare the notion of normal trees with the notion of normal aborescences,
which was introduced by Bürger and Melcher in [5]. We show that, given some set of vertices U ,
the existence of a normal arborescence containing U implies the existence of a normal tree
containing U . Further, we show that the converse is generally not true.
Normal trees and normal arborescences share the same separation property:
Proposition 3.1 ([5, Lemma 3.4]). Let D be a directed graph and let A be a normal arborescence
in D. For every strongly connected subgraph C of D and every two ≤A-incomparable elements
u, v ∈ V (C) ∩ V (A) there exists w ∈ V (C) such that w ≤A u, v, where ≤A refers to the tree order
of A.
As a normal arborescence is a subgraph of its host graph, it has more structural information
about connectivity in terms of directed paths from the root to its vertices. Nevertheless, this
comes with a less precise description of strong components: Given a normal arborescence A in D
and some a ∈ V (A), every strong component of D − ˚	⌈a⌉A intersecting ⌊a⌋A has to be contained in
⌊a⌋A by Proposition 3.1, but D[⌊a⌋A] does not have to be a strong component itself. In contrast
to this, given a normal spanning tree T of D, D[⌊t⌋T ] is a strong component of D − ˚	⌈t⌉T for
every t ∈ V (T ) by Proposition 2.1.

Proposition 3.2. There exists a directed graph with a normal spanning tree but without a normal
spanning arborescence.
Proof. Let D be the directed graph with V (D) := {aα : α < ω1} ∪ {bα : α < ω1} and
E(D) := {aαaβ : α < β < ω1} ∪ {bβ bα : α < β < ω1} ∪ {aαbα : α < ω1} ∪ {b0a0}.
First, we show that D − a0 is acyclic: Consider the unique linear order ≤ of V (D) satisfying
aα < aβ and bβ < bα for every α < β < ω1 and satisfying aα < bα′ for every α, α′ < ω1. Any edge
of E(D − a0) has the property that its tail precedes its head in ≤. Thus D − a0 is acyclic and
in particular, every strong component of D − a0 is a singleton. Thus the star with root a0 and
leaves in V (D − a0) is a normal spanning tree of D.
We suppose for a contradiction that D contains a normal spanning arborescence A. Let
γ < ω1 be such that either aγ or bγ is the root of A. The arborescence A contains uncountably
many edges in {aαbα : α < ω1}: Otherwise there exists γ < β < ω1 such that A contains no
edge in {aαbα : β ≤ α < ω1}. But every path from aγ or bγ to bβ has to contain an edge in
{aαbα : β ≤ α < ω1} and thus A is not spanning.
We consider the uncountable set {aα : aαbα ∈ E(A)}. Since the branches of A are countable,
there exist α′ < α′′ < ω1 with aα′ , aα′′ ∈ {aα : aαbα ∈ E(A)} such that aα′ , aα′′ are ≤A-
incomparable. The edges aα′ aα′′ and bα′′ bα′ connect ⌊aα′ ⌋A and ⌊aα′′ ⌋A in both directions,
contradicting the normality of A. This finishes the proof. □

4. Characterisation via metrizable space |D|
Diestel proved [6, Theorem 3.1] that the topological space |G| of an undirected graph G is
metrizable in the common topology MTop if and only if G has a normal spanning tree. For
directed graphs Bürger and Melcher established the space |D| together with the topology DTop;
see [4] for an introduction. In this section we show that Diestel’s characterisation carries over to
directed graphs:
Theorem 1.4. Let D be a directed graph. In DTop, |D| is metrizable if and only if D has a
normal spanning tree.
